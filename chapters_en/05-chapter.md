# Chapter 5: The Clockmaker's Workshop

The heavy hammered-iron door creaked open with a dull grind of internal gears and unlatched deadbolts. In the narrow crack, lit by the golden glow of a hanging acetylene lamp, appeared Kaelen's face.

The young master clockmaker had cheeks smudged with a fine dusting of coal soot, and his double-lens magnifying safety goggles were pushed up onto his forehead, holding back his messy brown hair. In his right hand, he held a heavy thirty-millimeter wrench forged from hardened steel. Upon recognizing Lira—dripping wet, her soaked satchel hugged tight—his usually calm, analytical eyes filled with sharp concern, quickly tempered by brotherly solidarity.

« You're completely out of your mind coming here at this hour, Lira », he whispered urgently, pulling her gently but firmly inside before slamming the reinforced door shut.

He threw three massive steel deadbolts and engaged a mechanical combination lock.

« Council militia raided two shops down the street since sunset », he continued, wiping his hands on his thick leather apron. « Searching turners' and mechanics' shops for astronomy books and un Guild-stamped clockwork parts. What happened at the Archives? »

Kaelen's workshop was a comforting sanctuary of ingenuity, warmth, and precision. The air carried a familiar scent of mineral oil, cleaning ether, brass filings, and strong black tea steeping on a small cast-iron stove. Copper piping ran along exposed red brick walls, feeding miniature pressure gauges that quivered under the steam of a small auxiliary boiler. On the large mahogany workbench covered in green felt, dozens of delicate cogs, anchor escapements, and quartz tuning forks rested under glass bell jars.

Lira set her waterlogged satchel down on the green felt and reverently pulled out the *Aethelgard Codex*. The antique rough leather glinted under the bright light of the acetylene burner.

Kaelen stepped closer, removed his work gloves, and gazed at the volume. For a fleeting moment, their hands touched—Lira's cool and trembling, Kaelen's warm and calloused.

« I took it out of Aisle 42 », she said, finally catching her breath. « And it's no useless star register, Kaelen. It's Aldous Vance's legacy. The vellum ink is made of synthetic amber dust and reactive copper. When exposed to a heat source and a specific sound frequency... the ink moves. »

Kaelen adjusted his magnifying goggles and leaned over the Codex, which Lira had just opened to page three hundred and forty-two. He stood speechless for several seconds, watching the golden script react to the stove's vibration.

« Good god... », he whispered, eyes wide. « These are blueprints for the old geothermal grid. Aldous didn't destroy the Precursor plants: he locked the main valves to keep the Council from turning them into weapons! »

He looked up at Lira, a newfound determination lighting up his face. « If the militia is looking for this book, they'll seal off the district any minute. We need to decode the first harmonic key before they blow my door down. »
