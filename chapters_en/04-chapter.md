# Chapter 4: Descent to the Lower City

The rain whipped against Lira's face with renewed force as she clung to the steel rungs anchored into the cliff's basalt stone. Every gust of autumn wind threatened to tear her from the slick wall, blowing her waterlogged wool coat around her and tangling her dark hair around her monocle.

Behind her, atop the precipice, the massive acetylene spotlights of the Council Palace swept the night sky with long yellowish beams, cutting through the mist to illuminate the stone spires and copper scaffolding of the Grand Archives. Steam whistles blared in the distance: Sergeant Thorne had sounded the general alarm. Tollgate guards were sealing the main gates of the upper city.

Lira began her descent down the Grand Staircase of the Hundred Steps, a monumental gangway of wrought iron and dark stone suspended over the empty gulf. The oxidized bronze handrails gushed with blackish runoff water. Every moss-and-oil-slicked step demanded absolute caution to avoid a fatal six-hundred-foot plunge into Boilermakers' Ravine.

The shift in environment was abrupt and suffocating the moment she passed through the heavy cast-iron gate of the lower tollhouse.

Gone were the quiet silence, clean slate, and sealed rapeseed oil lanterns of the High City. The Lower Town welcomed Lira with a perpetual roar of turbines, the thundering hissed discharges of pressure valves, and a stubborn stench of greasy coal steam, mineral oil, and stagnant water. Narrow alleys tangled like the intestines of a giant mechanical beast, lined with timber and corrugated iron hovels clinging to the stone arches of ancient Precursor aqueducts.

Lira immediately veered into Boilermakers' Alley, pulling her soaked coat collar up to her chin and hugging her canvas bag tight against her chest.

Here, under the reddish glow of street braziers and filthy acetylene lamps, late-night wanderers were few and far from friendly. Mechanics in grease-stained overalls trudged home from twelve-hour shifts, dry coughs rattling in their throats; ragpickers sorted piles of scrap copper and lead pipes scavenged from industrial dumps; and lookouts crouched under vaulted archways watched passersby with dark, calculating eyes.

« Hey, girl! » called out a stocky figure slouched against a cast-iron condensation tank. « What's a fine High City lady doing down in the soot at this hour? Lost your way or looking to buy contraband ether? »

Lira didn't even bother to glance his way. She quickened her pace over the broken cobbles, stepping around streams of polluted black water flowing from the Council's armament factories. She had known the Lower Town's layout since childhood; she knew which alleys to avoid to dodge militia patrols.

At the bend of Clockmakers' Street, at the heart of the precision craftsmen's quarter, the warm, flickering glow of a foundry forge illuminated the rutted street. Lira instantly recognized the forged-iron sign hanging over a stone archway: a winged gear intertwined with a steel tuning fork.

She darted under the archway, pressed her back against the workshop's reinforced iron door, and struck three crisp taps, followed by two spaced knocks. It was the secret code agreed upon for years between her and her most trusted ally.

The moment of truth had arrived: she had to convince Kaelen to risk his life alongside her.
