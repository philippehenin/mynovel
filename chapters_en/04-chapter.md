# Chapter 4: The Descent to the Lower Town

Rain stung Lira's face as she sprinted down the massive stairs of the Hundred-Step Stairway, her collar pulled up to her ears. The tarnished bronze handrails dripped with foul water, every slick step threatening to send her tumbling down the gorge. Behind her, atop the basalt cliff, the Council Palace searchlights swept the dark sky with yellow beams.

The change of scenery was abrupt the moment she passed the Tax Gate. Gone were the clean slates and sealed lanterns of the High City. The Lower Town reeked of coal steam, machine grease, and stagnant water. Narrow alleys tangled like the guts of a metal beast, lined with shacks clinging to ancient aqueduct frames.

Lira ducked into Boiler Alley, holding her bag tight. Night owls were rare and shady here: mechanics in grease-stained overalls, ragpickers sorting copper scraps, and spotters lurking under porches. But she knew the grid by heart.

« Hey, girl! What's a High City snob doing down in the soot at this hour? » called out a slumping figure against a water tank. Lira didn't even bother answering and quickened her pace, diving into the factory district maze.

At the bend of Clockmaker Street, the red glow of a forge lit up the broken pavement. Lira recognized the wrought-iron sign depicting a winged gear. She knocked three sharp times on the armored door of Kaelen's workshop. Moment of truth.
