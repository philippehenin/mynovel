# Chapter 3: The Council's Cops

The hobnailed boots of the Council's cops echoed against the slate tiles with the terrifying regularity of a guillotine blade. At every step, the steel fittings bit into the wet stone seeping from the glass windows, emitting a metallic clatter that made the shadows of the Grand Archives quiver.

Lira Vance crouched silently in the blind spot of a side aisle dedicated to tax ledgers from the ancient dynasty. Clutching her satchel strap against her chest to prevent the Codex from bumping against her ribs, she held her breath. Above her head, the pale, yellowish beam of a kerosene lantern suspended from a long pole swept across the spines of centuries-old bindings, sending billions of dust specks dancing in the air.

« I heard a click back toward the star chart section », grunted a harsh, cavernous voice, distorted by the copper diaphragm of a breathing mask.

It was Sergeant Thorne. Lira knew the man's terrifying reputation within the Council militia. Beneath his hammered copper mask and boiled leather cuirass studded with iron rivets, Thorne was no mere sadistic mercenary: he was a man broken by grief and resentment. Fifteen years earlier, his younger brother had perished, burned alive during the accidental explosion of the first geothermal prototype built by Aldous Vance in the Academy's basements. Since that tragic day, Thorne had devoted body and soul to the Inquisition of Silence, convinced that any ancient relic or technology brought nothing but death, chaos, and destruction.

« Search the alcoves », Thorne ordered his two guards. « If it's another fool apprentice digging for forbidden secrets to sell scrap copper down in the Lower Town, drag them straight to the purge pits in the purge tower. »

« Sir! » replied the militia guards, thumbing the hammers of their shock rifles.

Lira adjusted the strap of her tarred canvas satchel. The weight of the book was heavy, but abandoning Aldous's legacy was unthinkable. She slipped along the dark oak wainscoting without making a single rustle of fabric, utilizing the deep shadows cast by the basalt pillars to move toward the evacuation turret reserved for master binders.

At the bottom of the eastern wall, concealed behind a worn tapestry depicting the founding of the city, was a rectangular cast-iron ventilation hatch, caked with a century's worth of coal dust deposits.

Lira knelt quietly in the dust. Pulling her archivist's pocketknife from her coat, she inserted the fine blade between the hatch frame and the rusted brass latch. She pried the lever with extreme care. The metal gave a sharp squeak, but at that exact moment, one of Thorne's guards knocked over an entire stack of heavy leather ledgers at the far end of the gallery, completely drowning out the sound of the latch giving way.

Lira didn't hesitate for a second. She pushed open the iron grate, slid her satchel first into the narrow, dark conduit, and squeezed in headfirst, crawling on her stomach through the cold soot.

Just as she pulled the iron grate shut behind her, a cone of yellow light violently flooded the alcove she had just vacated. Thorne's boot crushed the cold blotter paper upon which her glass of tea had rested just minutes prior.

« There's warmth here », grunted the sergeant's voice from behind the stone wall. « The lamp wick is still smoking! Seal all exits to the High City! »

Lira didn't wait to hear more. Crawling frantically down the sloped ventilation shaft, she let the incline guide her outward. After sixty feet of a suffocating descent through coal dust, the icy, crisp night air of October slapped her square in the face.

She emerged onto a narrow corbeled ledge clinging to the sheer cliff that separated the High City from the precipice. Down below, drowning in a blanket of yellowish fog and streaked by the red glow of factory chimneys, the Lower Town spread out in an endless maze of tin roofs and iron footbridges. The Archives gates had just closed on her old life; her only lifeline now lay at the very bottom of the abyss.
