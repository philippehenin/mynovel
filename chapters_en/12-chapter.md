# Chapter 12: Exiting the Labyrinth

The south drainage tunnel sloped gently down through the bedrock of the Sandstone Mountains. Behind them, the roar of released steam and the thrum of piping drowned out the shouts of the militia and Thorne's shockfire blasts.

Lira and Kaelen ran through the muggy darkness for nearly twenty minutes without looking back, their footsteps echoing against the granite slabs. They moved blindly through the dark, guided only by the draft of warm air and the steady thrum of thermal pipes beneath their boots. Their shoulders brushed in the dark, bound by brotherly trust and a camaraderie forged in trial.

Suddenly, the wall of humid steam vanished, replaced by a blast of crystal-clear fresh air.

It was pure, crisp, invigorating air, completely free of any coal smoke or mineral ether smell. The evacuation tunnel opened onto a spectacular natural arch carved through centuries into the eastern face of the basalt cliff.

They emerged into the open air just as the first rays of the October sun broke through the sea of clouds.

Before their spellbound eyes spread the breathtaking vista of Shadowvale. It was a wild and majestic alpine valley, framed by misty crags and traversed by warm thermal streams sending up soft plumes of white steam. Amid century-old fir trees stood the grand ruins of the Ancients' geothermal plants: pink stone cooling towers and ivy-covered aqueduct bridges.

Far away on the western horizon, Aethelgard's slate roofs and belching chimneys looked tiny and insignificant, smothered beneath their ink-black smog layer.

Lira leaned against the rock face, taking in the magnificent panorama with an exhausted but triumphant smile. For the first time in her life, she breathed air untainted by soot.

« We made it through the first stage », she said softly, adjusting her monocle.

Kaelen placed his hand over hers, his brown eyes reflecting the morning's golden light. « This is only the beginning, Lira. Thorne and the Council won't wait long to deploy their airships. But the mountains are vast, and the Codex shows us the way. »
